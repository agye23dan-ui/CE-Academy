# Calculus I: 14-Week Study Plan and Resources

Time budget: 8 to 10 hours per week (3 hours reading and notes, 4 to 5 hours problems, 1 hour review with the app's quizzes).

## Textbooks
- James Stewart, *Calculus: Early Transcendentals* (Cengage). The standard university text, with many graded problems.
- Michael Spivak, *Calculus*. Proof-based. Use it for the epsilon-delta chapters if you want rigour.
- Gilbert Strang, *Calculus* (free from MIT OpenCourseWare). Strong on intuition and applications.
- Paul's Online Math Notes (Calculus I), free, with many solved examples.

## Free courses
- MIT OpenCourseWare 18.01SC Single Variable Calculus (lectures, problem sets, exams with solutions).
- Khan Academy: AP Calculus AB/BC for extra practice.
- 3Blue1Brown, *Essence of Calculus* (visual intuition, watch in week 1).

## Week by week

**Week 1: Functions and limits (Ch 1, 2.1 to 2.4).** Objectives: compute limits algebraically; use one-sided limits; apply the squeeze theorem. Do: Assignment 1 Q1. Checkpoint: 15 limits in 30 minutes without error.

**Week 2: Epsilon-delta, trig limits, infinity (Ch 2.2, 2.5, 2.6).** Objectives: write a δ-ε proof; find asymptotes. Do: Assignment 1 Q2. Checkpoint: prove lim x^2 = 4 cold.

**Week 3: Continuity and IVT (Ch 3).** Objectives: classify discontinuities; use the IVT for root existence. Do: Assignment 1 Q3, Q4.

**Week 4: The derivative (Ch 4.1 to 4.3).** Objectives: derive derivatives from the definition; master product, quotient, chain rules. Do: Assignment 1 Q5 to Q8. Checkpoint: differentiate 20 mixed functions in 25 minutes.

**Week 5: Implicit and logarithmic differentiation, higher derivatives (Ch 4.4 to 4.6).** Do: remaining Assignment 1 items; sit the **Class Test** (time it, 60 minutes).

**Week 6: Related rates and linearisation (Ch 5.1, 5.2).** Do: Assignment 2 Q1 to Q4. Tip: draw a diagram first and substitute numbers only after differentiating.

**Week 7: MVT and curve sketching (Ch 5.3, 5.4).** Do: Assignment 2 Q5, Q6. Checkpoint: sketch three cubic and rational functions with full analysis.

**Week 8: Optimisation, L'Hôpital, Newton (Ch 5.5 to 5.7).** Do: Assignment 2 Q7 to Q9. Review for the **midterm**: sit the first half of the Final Exam as a mock.

**Week 9: Antiderivatives and the definite integral (Ch 5.8, 6.1).** Objectives: Riemann sums, properties. Do: Assignment 3 Q1.

**Week 10: FTC and substitution (Ch 6.2, 6.3).** Do: Assignment 3 Q2, Q3. Checkpoint: 15 substitution integrals in 40 minutes.

**Week 11: Integration by parts and other techniques (Ch 6.4 to 6.6).** Do: Assignment 3 Q4.

**Week 12: Applications of integration (Ch 7).** Do: Assignment 3 Q5, Q6. Practise choosing between discs, washers and shells.

**Week 13: Sequences and series (Ch 8.1 to 8.2).** Do: Assignment 3 Q7. Build a one-page "which test do I use" flowchart.

**Week 14: Power series, Taylor (Ch 8.3, 8.4) and final revision.** Do: Assignment 3 Q8, Q9; redo the questions you missed on the **Class Test**, then sit the full **Final Exam** under exam conditions (3 hours, no notes).

## Milestone checklist
- [ ] I can state the epsilon-delta definition and prove a linear and a quadratic limit.
- [ ] I can differentiate any elementary function without a table.
- [ ] I can set up and solve a related rates and an optimisation problem.
- [ ] I can sketch a function using f' and f''.
- [ ] I can integrate by substitution and by parts, and use FTC with variable limits.
- [ ] I can choose the right convergence test for a series and justify it.
- [ ] I can write the Maclaurin series of e^x, sin x, cos x, 1/(1 - x), ln(1 + x) from memory.
- [ ] I scored at least 70 percent on the Final Exam with no notes.

## Study habits that work
1. Rework every solved example in the notes with the page covered.
2. Keep an error log: for each mistake write the correct step and the rule you broke. Review it weekly.
3. Use the app's quiz on weak topics every few days (spaced repetition beats cramming).
4. Teach a topic aloud for five minutes each week. If you stumble, that is the gap to fix.
