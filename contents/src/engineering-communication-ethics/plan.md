# Engineering Communication and Ethics: 14-Week Study Plan and Resources

Time budget: 6 to 8 hours per week (2 reading, 4 writing and analysis, 1 review with the app's quizzes). This course is learned by **writing and discussing**, so keep a portfolio folder with every draft, the feedback you receive and your revisions.

## Textbooks and references
- Gerald Alred, Charles Brusaw and Walter Oliu, *Handbook of Technical Writing* (St. Martin's). A comprehensive reference for documents and style.
- Mike Markel, *Technical Communication* (Bedford/St. Martin's). Chapters on audience, reports, proposals and presentations.
- William Strunk and E. B. White, *The Elements of Style*; Joseph Williams, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*. For clear sentences.
- Charles Harris, Michael Davis, Michael Pritchard and Michael Rabins, *Engineering Ethics: Concepts and Cases* (Cengage). The standard case-based text.
- Mike Martin and Roland Schinzinger, *Ethics in Engineering*.
- Nancy Leveson, *Engineering a Safer World* and "An Investigation of the Therac-25 Accidents" (Leveson and Turner, 1993). For safety and software case studies.
- Diane Vaughan, *The Challenger Launch Decision* (normalisation of deviance).
- The IEEE Code of Ethics, the NSPE Code of Ethics for Engineers and the ACM Code of Ethics (all free online); the IEEE Editorial Style Manual for references.
- Your national engineering body's code of conduct (for example the Ghana Institution of Engineering) and the national standards authority's catalogue.

## Free resources
- Online Ethics Center (National Academy of Engineering) for case studies.
- Purdue OWL for writing, citation and grammar.
- Standards from IEEE Xplore (through your library), ISO and IEC previews, and RFCs from the IETF (free).
- MIT OpenCourseWare technical and scientific writing courses (search the 21W subjects); Toastmasters-style practice for speaking.

## Week by week

**Week 1: Why communication and ethics matter (Ch 1.1, 1.2, 6.1).** Read two short case summaries (Challenger and Therac-25). Write a 300-word reflection: where did communication or ethics fail? Do: Assignment 1 Q1.

**Week 2: Style, clarity and units (Ch 1.3, 1.4).** Do: Assignment 1 Q1, Q5. Habit: rewrite five sentences from your own previous coursework each day.

**Week 3: Visuals and tables (Ch 1.5).** Do: Assignment 1 Q6. Redraw three figures from your lab reports to meet every rule in the checklist.

**Week 4: Reports, abstracts and the IMRaD structure (Ch 2.1, 2.2).** Do: Assignment 1 Q2. Write a one-page summary of a lab you completed. Sit the **Class Test** at the end of the week.

**Week 5: Requirements, proposals and professional correspondence (Ch 2.3, 2.4).** Do: Assignment 1 Q3, Q4.

**Week 6: Citation, plagiarism and licences (Ch 3).** Do: Assignment 1 Q7 and Assignment 3 Q6. Practise IEEE referencing with ten sources.

**Week 7: Presentations and teamwork (Ch 4).** Prepare and deliver a 5-minute talk on a project; record it and review. Write a team agreement.

**Week 8: Standards: what, who and how (Ch 5.1 to 5.4).** Do: Assignment 2 Q1, Q2, Q3, Q8. Mock midterm: first half of the Final Exam.

**Week 9: Risk, safety and reliability (Ch 5.5).** Do: Assignment 2 Q4 to Q7. Perform an FMEA on a small device you own.

**Week 10: Ethical theories and codes (Ch 6.2 to 6.4).** Do: Assignment 3 Q1. Apply the seven-step method to two cases.

**Week 11: Professional responsibilities: honesty, conflicts of interest, whistleblowing (Ch 7.1 to 7.3).** Do: Assignment 3 Q3, Q4, Q5.

**Week 12: IP, privacy, security, sustainability, AI (Ch 7.4 to 7.6).** Do: Assignment 3 Q6, Q7, Q8.

**Week 13: Case studies (Ch 8).** Do: Assignment 3 Q2, Q9. Write a full case analysis (about 1,200 words) of one case not covered in the notes (for example Toyota unintended acceleration or a local infrastructure failure), with references.

**Week 14: Revision.** Redo the missed Class Test questions, then sit the **Final Exam** under exam conditions (3 hours, closed book except for a copy of the IEEE and NSPE codes).

## Milestone checklist
- [ ] I can state the audience, purpose and main message of any document before writing it.
- [ ] I can write a clear abstract, a testable requirement and a professional email.
- [ ] I can produce a correct graph or table with caption, units and references to it.
- [ ] I can cite sources in IEEE style, and I can recognise plagiarism.
- [ ] I can find, read and apply a standard, distinguishing normative and informative text.
- [ ] I can estimate and reduce risk using a risk matrix, the hierarchy of controls and FMEA.
- [ ] I can analyse a dilemma with several ethical theories and the codes, and defend a decision.
- [ ] I can describe at least five engineering failures and their ethical and communication lessons.
- [ ] I scored at least 70 percent on the Final Exam.

## Study habits that work
1. Write something every day, even 200 words, and let it rest overnight before revising.
2. Read your draft aloud; stumbling shows where the sentence is unclear.
3. Ask a friend from another discipline to read your document. If they cannot find the main point in 30 seconds, restructure.
4. For ethics cases, always separate the facts from the judgements, and argue the other side's best case before deciding.
5. Keep a list of ethics "tests" (publicity, role model, reversibility) and apply them each time.
6. Join a professional society (IEEE student branch, your national engineering body) and read its code and its news.
