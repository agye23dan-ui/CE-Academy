# Engineering Communication and Ethics: Final Examination

Duration: 3 hours. Closed book (a copy of the IEEE and NSPE codes is provided). Total: 100 marks.
- Section A: 20 multiple-choice questions, 1 mark each (20 marks). Answer all.
- Section B: 8 short questions, 5 marks each (40 marks). Answer all.
- Section C: 5 long questions, 10 marks each. Answer any four (40 marks).

## Section A: Multiple Choice (20 marks)

## Section C: Long Questions (answer any four, 10 marks each)

**C1. Abstract critique and rewrite.** A student wrote this abstract: "This report is about a project that we did on temperature measurement. We used a sensor and a microcontroller. It worked well. Many tests were done. The results were good and the project was a success."
(a) Identify at least five defects. [4]
(b) Rewrite the abstract (120 to 160 words) using these facts: a digital temperature sensor (DS18B20) connected to a microcontroller was evaluated against a calibrated reference thermometer at 20 test points from 0 to 60 °C; the mean absolute error was 0.21 °C and the maximum error was 0.38 °C; the specified accuracy was ±0.5 °C; the response time was 4.1 s; the power consumption was 12 mW. [6]

**C2. Standards and compliance.** A company plans to sell a wrist-worn device that measures heart rate. The marketing team wants to describe it as a "general wellness" gadget; the product team wants to add a feature that detects atrial fibrillation and alerts the user.
(a) Explain why the intended use affects the regulatory path. [2]
(b) List at least five standards or regulatory requirements that would apply if the device is a medical device and say what each addresses (for example IEC 60601-1, IEC 62304, ISO 14971, ISO 13485, IEC 60601-1-2 for EMC, biocompatibility ISO 10993, battery safety IEC 62133). [5]
(c) Give a three-row hazard table (hazard, cause, severity 1 to 5, likelihood 1 to 5, risk, control) for the alerting feature. [3]

**C3. Boeing 737 MAX analysis.** Using the case facts in the notes:
(a) State the technical design decisions that increased risk. [3]
(b) Identify the organisational and communication failures. [3]
(c) Identify at least four canons of the IEEE or NSPE codes relevant to the engineers and managers involved and explain how. [4]

**C4. Health data ethics.** A start-up has a free fitness app that collects heart rate, location and sleep data. The founders propose selling "anonymised" aggregate data to insurance companies.
(a) Identify at least five ethical and legal issues. [4]
(b) Explain why "anonymised" data is not automatically safe. [2]
(c) Analyse the proposal using two ethical theories and recommend a course of action with design safeguards. [4]

**C5. Integrated scenario.** A student team builds a delivery robot to be tested on a university campus. Two weeks before the demonstration, a test shows that the robot does not reliably stop for pedestrians who step in front of it within 1 m at full speed (0.8 m/s): in 3 of 20 trials it stopped too late. The faculty sponsor wants the demo to go ahead as planned.
(a) Write three measurable safety requirements for pedestrian protection. [3]
(b) Propose a hazard analysis (identify hazard, severity, likelihood, risk before and after controls) and apply the hierarchy of controls. [3]
(c) Decide whether the demonstration should proceed, and justify your decision with at least two ethical lenses and the codes. [2]
(d) Write a two-sentence message to the sponsor. [2]

## Section B solutions

**B1.** Subject: **"Burn-in test: 2 of 10 units failed, solder defect identified, rework plan proposed."** Opening: "The 24-hour burn-in test of 10 units finished this morning: 8 passed and 2 failed (units 4 and 7). Inspection shows cold solder joints on connector J3 in both failed units. I propose reworking all remaining boards and repeating the burn-in, which will add three days to the schedule; I would like your approval by Thursday." (Marks: 1 subject, 1 result and numbers, 1 cause, 1 action and impact, 1 professional tone.)

**B2.** (a) **Table:** exact values need to be read. (b) **Line graph:** a trend over time (continuous variable). (c) **Block diagram:** shows structure and signal flow. (d) **Bar chart** (or table): compares a single quantity across a small number of discrete categories. (1 mark for format, brief justification for each, 0.25 each.)

**B3.** **Not acceptable.** It is plagiarism (presenting another person's work as your own) unless attribution is given and the course permits it; it is also a **licensing** matter: forum code usually carries a licence (for example Stack Overflow content is under Creative Commons BY-SA, which requires attribution and share-alike), so copying without credit breaches the licence. The student should have **cited the source in a comment and in the report**, checked the licence and the course rules, understood and tested the code, and preferably written the solution in their own words. (2 marks for plagiarism, 2 for licensing, 1 for the correct practice.)

**B4.** Step 1: ISO 26262 is the **functional safety standard for road vehicles** (electrical/electronic systems), adapted from IEC 61508.
Step 2: **ASIL** (Automotive Safety Integrity Level) classifies the risk of a hazard by severity, exposure and controllability: **QM** (quality management, no ASIL), **A, B, C, D** (D is the most stringent).
Step 3: As the ASIL rises, the development process requires more rigour: stronger verification methods and coverage metrics (for example MC/DC in software for ASIL D), more independent reviews and assessment, redundancy and diagnostic coverage, and fuller documentation and traceability (1 mark scope, 2 for ASIL and levels, 2 for process changes.)

**B5.** (a) Step 1: series: R = 0.98³ = 0.98 × 0.98 × 0.98 = **0.941** (about 94.1 percent).
(b) Step 2: parallel: R = 1 − (1 − 0.90)² = 1 − 0.01 = **0.99**.
(c) **Common-cause failures** (the same defect, same power supply, same software bug, same environmental event) break the independence assumption, so the real reliability is lower. (Other valid reasons: failure of the switching or voting mechanism.)

**B6.** (a) **Utilitarianism.** (b) **Deontology** (or rights-based ethics). (c) **Virtue ethics.** (d) **Justice as fairness (Rawls).** (e) **Ethics of care.** (1 mark each.)

**B7.** (1) Verify the facts and your competence. (2) Raise the concern internally in writing, first with your supervisor. (3) Escalate through further internal channels (higher management, ethics office, ombudsperson). (4) If the danger is serious and internal channels fail, report to the appropriate external authority (regulator, professional body), after seeking advice. (5) Keep records and protect evidence throughout, and seek legal and professional support. (1 mark each, order matters.)

**B8.** Model answer: "Therac-25 was a computer-controlled radiation therapy machine (1985 to 1987). Software race conditions, together with the removal of the hardware interlocks used on its predecessor, allowed the beam to deliver massive overdoses, killing or injuring several patients. Organisationally, the manufacturer had no independent software review, dismissed early reports, gave operators cryptic error messages and was slow to investigate. Safety-critical systems need defence in depth and a culture that investigates every incident." (1 mark per sentence plus 1 for accuracy.)

## Section C solutions

**C1.**
(a) Defects: no context or problem statement; "a sensor" and "a microcontroller" are unspecified; no objective; no method (what was tested and how); no quantitative results ("worked well", "good" are vague); no conclusion tied to the specification; passive "many tests were done" hides the actor and number; the first sentence ("This report is about...") wastes words; no mention of limitations. (Any five, with 0.8 marks each.)
(b) **Model rewrite (about 135 words):** "Low-power temperature monitoring requires sensors that meet a stated accuracy at modest cost. This study evaluated the DS18B20 digital temperature sensor, connected to a microcontroller, against a calibrated reference thermometer. Twenty test points between 0 and 60 °C were measured in a stirred bath. The mean absolute error was 0.21 °C and the maximum error was 0.38 °C, within the specified accuracy of ±0.5 °C. The response time was 4.1 s, and the circuit consumed 12 mW. These results show that the sensor and microcontroller combination meets the accuracy requirement across the tested range and is suitable for slowly changing temperatures, but its 4.1 s response time limits its use for rapid transients." Checklist (1 mark each, 6): background/problem; objective; method with test range and number of points; the two error values; comparison with specification; response time and power; conclusion; length and standalone.

**C2.**
(a) Regulators judge a product by its **intended use** and the claims made (including marketing and the software features). A heart-rate display for general wellness is typically low-risk and lightly regulated, whereas a feature that **detects a disease (atrial fibrillation) and alerts the user** is a diagnostic claim, so the device is a medical device (for example FDA clearance in the US, or CE marking under the EU Medical Device Regulation), with much stricter requirements, clinical evidence and post-market surveillance.
(b) Examples: **ISO 13485** (quality management system for medical devices); **ISO 14971** (risk management for medical devices); **IEC 62304** (medical device software life cycle, with software safety classes A, B, C); **IEC 60601-1** (basic safety and essential performance of medical electrical equipment); **IEC 60601-1-2** (EMC for medical electrical equipment); **ISO 10993** series (biocompatibility of skin-contact materials); **IEC 62133** (safety of the lithium battery) and **IEC 62366** (usability engineering); plus data protection law for health data. (1 mark each for five items with their scope.)
(c) Sample table:
- **Hazard:** false negative (missed atrial fibrillation). **Cause:** algorithm error, poor skin contact or motion artefact. **S:** 4, **L:** 3, **Risk:** 12. **Controls:** validation on a clinical dataset, signal-quality check, labelling that the device is not a substitute for medical advice.
- **Hazard:** false positive alert causing anxiety and unnecessary treatment. **Cause:** artefact, algorithm threshold. **S:** 3, **L:** 3, **Risk:** 9. **Controls:** confirmation step, clear wording, referral to a clinician.
- **Hazard:** skin burn or irritation. **Cause:** battery or sensor heating, allergic reaction. **S:** 4, **L:** 1, **Risk:** 4. **Controls:** temperature limit, battery protection circuit, biocompatible materials.
(1 mark per row with a sensible hazard, scores and control.)

**C3.**
(a) Design decisions: MCAS relied on **a single angle-of-attack sensor**, so one faulty sensor could trigger repeated nose-down commands; MCAS authority was increased during development (it could move the stabiliser by a larger amount than first planned) and could act repeatedly; the failure condition was not classified at the correct severity; there was no AOA disagree alert as a standard feature; and the design assumed that pilots would react within a short time to a runaway trim. (b) Organisational and communication failures: pressure to avoid costly retraining and to compete with a rival aircraft; limited disclosure of MCAS to pilots, airlines and, in part, to the regulator; reliance on the manufacturer to certify its own designs (delegated authority); inadequate escalation of internal concerns; a culture that prioritised schedule and cost. (c) Canons: **hold paramount the safety, health and welfare of the public** (NSPE 1, IEEE 1); **issue public statements only in an objective and truthful manner** and **be honest and realistic in stating claims** (NSPE 3, IEEE 3: statements about MCAS and training needs); **perform services only in areas of competence** and **disclose promptly factors that might endanger the public** (IEEE 1, NSPE 2); **avoid deceptive acts** (NSPE 5); **support colleagues in following the code** and **seek, accept and offer honest criticism of technical work** (IEEE 7 and 10: encourage speaking up). (Marks: 3 + 3 + 4, 1 per canon with a link to the case.)

**C4.**
(a) Issues: **informed consent** (users may not realise their data will be sold); **privacy of sensitive health and location data**; **re-identification risk**; **discrimination** (insurers could raise premiums or deny cover); **data security and breaches**; **legal compliance** (GDPR: lawful basis, purpose limitation, data minimisation, right to erasure; health-data rules); **fairness and power imbalance** (a free app, users pay with their data); **transparency**. (b) Anonymised data can often be re-identified by linking it with other datasets (for example location traces uniquely identify people); small groups in "aggregate" data can reveal individuals; and combining data sources enables inference. Genuine anonymisation requires techniques such as aggregation with minimum group sizes, differential privacy, and strict contractual and technical controls. (c) **Utilitarian:** possible benefits (funding the service, research insights, cheaper insurance for healthy people) versus harms (privacy loss, discrimination); the harms fall on vulnerable users and are hard to reverse, so the benefits are unlikely to outweigh them without safeguards. **Rights-based/deontological:** users have a right to control sensitive personal data and not to be used merely as a means of profit; consent must be explicit, specific and freely given. **Recommendation:** do not sell data to insurers; if monetisation is required, use opt-in consent, offer paid ad-free tiers, share only aggregated and differentially private statistics for research with ethics review, provide deletion and export, publish a clear policy and appoint a data protection officer. (Marks: 4 + 2 + 4.)

**C5.**
(a) Sample requirements: "The robot shall come to a complete stop within 0.5 m (measured from the sensing of an obstacle) when an obstacle appears at 1.0 m at the maximum speed of 0.8 m/s, in 100 out of 100 consecutive trials." "The robot shall limit its speed to 0.5 m/s whenever pedestrians are detected within 5 m." "The robot shall provide a hardware emergency stop button reachable on the vehicle and a remote emergency stop with a response time under 100 ms." (1 mark each: measurable, with numbers, units, conditions.)
(b) Hazard: **collision with a pedestrian at close range**. Before controls: severity 3 (injury, minor to moderate), likelihood 3 (3 out of 20 trials failed), risk 9. Controls by level: **eliminate/substitute**: demonstrate in a closed area with no pedestrians; **engineering**: reduce speed to 0.4 m/s, add a bumper contact switch cutting motor power, add a second (independent) sensor, a hardware E-stop; **administrative**: safety marshals, barriers, a trained operator holding a remote E-stop, written test procedure; **PPE**: not applicable to bystanders (high-visibility vests for staff). After controls: severity 2, likelihood 1, risk 2. (3 marks.)
(c) **Decision:** the demonstration should not proceed in the planned form (full speed near pedestrians); it may proceed in a modified, controlled form after re-testing. **Consequentialist:** a collision could injure someone and damage the team's and university's reputation; the cost of delay or modification is small. **Rights/deontological:** pedestrians have not consented to be test subjects. **Codes:** the public's safety is paramount; engineers must disclose known risks and are accountable for what they sign off. (2 marks.)
(d) Model message: "Our latest test shows the robot failed to stop in time in 3 of 20 trials at full speed, so I recommend we do not demonstrate it near pedestrians until we have reduced speed and added an independent bumper stop and retested. We can still hold the demonstration in a barrier-protected area with a safety marshal and a remote emergency stop, and I will send you the test report by Friday." (1 mark for the clear facts and recommendation, 1 mark for alternative and tone.)
