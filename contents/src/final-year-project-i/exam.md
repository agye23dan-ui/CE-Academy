## Section B: Short Questions (40 marks)

**B1. Scope (5 marks)** For a project "A low-cost air-quality node", list three items in scope, three out of scope and two assumptions, and explain why defining scope matters.

**B2. Requirements (5 marks)** Rewrite "the system must be reliable" and "the display must be readable" as verifiable requirements with a test method for each.

**B3. Screening (5 marks)** A search returns 2,000 records; 500 pass the title screen, 100 the abstract screen and 25 are read in full. Compute each stage's yield and the overall yield, and the hours needed at 2.5 hours per full paper.

**B4. Decision matrix (5 marks)** Criteria weights: cost 0.4, performance 0.3, risk 0.2, time 0.1. Scores (cost, performance, risk, time): P = (6, 8, 7, 5), Q = (9, 5, 6, 7), R = (7, 7, 8, 8). Compute the weighted scores and choose; and the winner with equal weights.

**B5. Float (5 marks)** Explain the difference between the critical path and a near-critical path and why managers monitor both. What does negative float mean?

**B6. PERT (5 marks)** A task has optimistic 2, most likely 4 and pessimistic 8 weeks. Compute its expected duration and standard deviation, and explain why the expected value is greater than the most likely value.

**B7. Hypothesis test (5 marks)** Two filters are compared with 10 runs each: A has mean accuracy 52.0 and s = 4.0; B has mean 55.0 and s = 4.0. Compute t = difference/√(sA²/n + sB²/n), compare with 2.10 (critical value, 18 degrees of freedom, 5 percent two-sided) and conclude. What would you do next?

**B8. Ethics (5 marks)** A student uses survey data from classmates without telling them the purpose and keeps names in the spreadsheet. List the ethical problems and the corrective actions.

### Section B

**B1.** In scope (examples): sensing PM2.5, temperature and humidity; wireless reporting to a dashboard; calibration against a reference instrument. Out of scope: a consumer mobile app, mass production and regulatory certification, indoor-air purification. Assumptions: mains or USB power is available; network coverage at the test site [3]. Defining scope sets boundaries so the work fits the time and budget, avoids scope creep, and gives a basis for evaluation and for saying "no" to additions [2].

**B2.** "The node shall run for 30 days continuously without a reset, and report at least 99 percent of its scheduled readings; verified by a 30-day soak test with logging" [2.5]. "The display shall show characters of at least 5 mm height with a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 and be readable from 1 m in 500 lux ambient light; verified by readability tests with five users and a lux meter" [2.5].

**B3.** Title screen 500/2000 = **25 percent**; abstract screen 100/500 = **20 percent**; full-text 25/100 = **25 percent**; overall 25/2000 = **1.25 percent** [3]. Reading time = 25 × 2.5 = **62.5 hours** [2].

**B4.** P = 0.4·6 + 0.3·8 + 0.2·7 + 0.1·5 = 2.4 + 2.4 + 1.4 + 0.5 = **6.7**; Q = 3.6 + 1.5 + 1.2 + 0.7 = **7.0**; R = 2.8 + 2.1 + 1.6 + 0.8 = **7.3**; choose **R** [3]. Equal weights: P = 6.5, Q = 6.75, R = **7.5**; R wins in both, so the choice is robust [2].

**B5.** The critical path is the longest path with zero float; a near-critical path has small positive float and becomes critical after a small delay [2]. Managers monitor both because delays on near-critical paths can quickly create a new critical path [1]. Negative float means the schedule is already behind the target date (the task's latest finish is earlier than its forecast finish): corrective action is required [2].

**B6.** Expected = (2 + 4 × 4 + 8)/6 = **4.33 weeks**; σ = (8 - 2)/6 = **1.0 week** [3]. The distribution is skewed: the pessimistic value is much further from the most likely value than the optimistic one, so the mean lies above the mode [2].

**B7.** Standard error = √(16/10 + 16/10) = √3.2 = 1.789; t = 3.0/1.789 = **1.68** [3]. Since 1.68 < 2.10 the difference is **not statistically significant** at the 5 percent level (p ≈ 0.11) [1]. Next: collect more runs (power analysis), report the confidence interval and effect size, and check whether a 3-point difference matters in practice [1].

**B8.** Problems: no informed consent or disclosure of purpose; personally identifiable information stored without need; possible lack of ethical approval; risk of misuse or disclosure [3]. Corrections: obtain approval, give an information sheet and consent form, collect anonymously or remove names, store data securely, delete after the retention period, and report in aggregate [2].
