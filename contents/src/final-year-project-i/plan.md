# Final Year Project I: 14-Week Study Plan and Resources

Time budget: 10 to 14 hours per week on your project (about 2 hours of meetings, reading and writing, and the rest on design, simulation and preliminary experiments), plus 1 hour reviewing these notes with the app's quizzes. The plan below is a model timeline for one semester; adjust it to your institution's milestone dates and your supervisor's advice.

## Books and guides
- Gerard Voland, *Engineering by Design*; Karl Ulrich and Steven Eppinger, *Product Design and Development*; George Dieter and Linda Schmidt, *Engineering Design* (concept generation, selection, requirements).
- Colin Neville, *The Complete Guide to Referencing and Avoiding Plagiarism*; Kate Turabian and the IEEE Editorial Style Manual for citation.
- Nigel Gilbert and Paul Stoneman on research methods; Justin Zobel, *Writing for Computer Science*; Strunk and White or Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style*, for clear writing.
- Project Management Institute, *PMBOK Guide* (planning, risk, earned value); Eric Verzuh, *The Fast Forward MBA in Project Management*.
- Douglas Montgomery, *Design and Analysis of Experiments*, and John Taylor, *An Introduction to Error Analysis*, for measurement and statistics.

## Free resources
- University writing centres and library guides on literature searching, referencing and avoiding plagiarism.
- IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar, Scopus and ResearchGate for papers; Zotero, Mendeley or BibTeX for reference management; Overleaf (LaTeX) or a word processor with a thesis template.
- Git and GitHub/GitLab for version control; Notion, Trello or GitHub Projects for task boards; ProjectLibre or a spreadsheet for Gantt charts and critical-path calculation.
- NIST and ISO/IEC/IEEE standard summaries; the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals pages for impact framing.
- Young Engineers' competitions, past FYP archives in your library (read two good reports to see the expected level).

## Week by week

**Week 1: Topic, supervisor and problem.** Brainstorm topics, check feasibility, meet potential supervisors, and agree a meeting schedule. Deliverable: a one-page project idea (problem, users, gap, feasibility, what you will deliver).

**Week 2: Problem statement, aim and objectives.** Write the statement with a measurable target; draft SMART objectives and a scope with out-of-scope items and assumptions. Deliverable: the problem statement, aim and objectives agreed with the supervisor.

**Week 3: Literature search.** Design Boolean search strings, search at least three databases, log the searches, and begin screening. Deliverable: a search log and a screening table (records, stage counts, yield).

**Week 4: Reading and synthesis.** Read 20 to 40 papers in passes; fill a synthesis matrix; draft the review's structure and the research gap. Deliverable: the synthesis matrix and a review outline.

**Week 5: Requirements.** Identify stakeholders, write measurable requirements, apply MoSCoW, check standards and constraints, create the traceability table. Deliverable: the requirements specification v1.

**Week 6: Concepts and selection.** Generate alternatives for each subsystem, define criteria and weights, build a decision matrix and test its sensitivity. Deliverable: a concept-selection document with the rationale.

**Week 7: Proposal draft and midterm review.** Assemble the proposal (background, objectives, review summary, method, plan, budget, risks, ethics). Deliverable: the full proposal draft for supervisor feedback.

**Week 8: Architecture and interfaces.** Block diagrams, data flows, interface definitions, power and timing budgets, UML if software. Deliverable: the architecture document.

**Week 9: Modelling, simulation and feasibility.** Build a simulation or a proof-of-concept spike of the riskiest part; check the results against theory or a published reference. Deliverable: preliminary results with numbers and uncertainty.

**Week 10: Planning.** Work breakdown structure, effort estimates with contingency, Gantt chart, critical path, PERT for uncertain tasks, milestones, and a plan for earned-value tracking. Deliverable: the project plan with a critical path.

**Week 11: Risk, budget and procurement.** Risk register with scores and mitigations, bill of materials with 15 to 20 percent contingency, long-lead orders placed. Deliverable: the risk register and budget.

**Week 12: Ethics, safety and test plan.** Ethical approval if needed, hazard assessment, data management plan, verification and validation test plan linked to the requirements. Deliverable: the ethics/safety statement and the test plan.

**Week 13: Final report and presentation preparation.** Complete the report (introduction, review, requirements, design, plan, preliminary results, conclusions), proofread, check references, rehearse the presentation. Deliverable: the FYP I report and slides.

**Week 14: Presentation and hand-over to FYP II.** Present, answer questions, record feedback, and revise the plan for FYP II (a task list for the first four weeks). Deliverable: the presentation, the final report, and the revised FYP II plan.

## Assessment pattern used in this course
Three assignments (proposal and literature review; requirements, concept selection and reliability; planning, cost, statistics and professional practice), a class test, and a three-hour final. Reproduce every calculation in the notes (critical path, PERT, earned value, NPV, sample size, uncertainty) with your own project data.

## Study tips
- Write something every week; the report is the project's memory.
- Prove the riskiest idea early with a small experiment before committing the whole plan to it.
- Keep every decision, assumption and number in the logbook, with the date and the reason.
- Report numbers with uncertainty and a baseline; avoid "better" without a measured comparison.
- Meet your supervisor with a short written update every time, and raise problems the week you see them.
