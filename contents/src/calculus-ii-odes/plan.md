# Calculus II and Differential Equations: 14-Week Study Plan and Resources

Time budget: 9 to 11 hours per week (3 reading, 5 to 6 problems, 1 review with the app's quizzes). This course has more computation than Calculus I: set aside time for a fixed daily block of problem-solving, and use a computer algebra system (SymPy, Wolfram Alpha) only to **check** answers, never to produce them.

## Textbooks
- James Stewart, *Calculus: Early Transcendentals* (Cengage), the later chapters on techniques, parametric curves, vectors, partial derivatives, multiple integrals and vector calculus.
- Boyce and DiPrima, *Elementary Differential Equations and Boundary Value Problems*. The standard ODE text with many applications.
- Zill, *A First Course in Differential Equations with Modeling Applications*. Gentler, with many worked examples.
- Marsden and Tromba, *Vector Calculus*; Schey, *Div, Grad, Curl, and All That* (an intuitive introduction to vector calculus).
- Kreyszig, *Advanced Engineering Mathematics* (for Laplace transforms and engineering applications).
- Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos* (later reading on phase planes).

## Free courses
- MIT OpenCourseWare 18.02SC Multivariable Calculus (lectures, problem sets, exams).
- MIT OpenCourseWare 18.03SC Differential Equations.
- Paul's Online Math Notes: Calculus II, Calculus III and Differential Equations.
- 3Blue1Brown videos on differential equations and the Laplace transform; Khan Academy multivariable calculus.

## Week by week

**Week 1: Integration techniques (Ch 1.1 to 1.4).** Do: Assignment 1 Q1. Checkpoint: choose a technique for 20 mixed integrals within 10 seconds each, then solve them.

**Week 2: Improper integrals and parametric curves (Ch 1.5, 2.1).** Do: Assignment 1 Q2, Q3.

**Week 3: Polar coordinates (Ch 2.2).** Do: Assignment 1 Q4. Practise sketching r = f(θ).

**Week 4: Vectors, lines, planes, surfaces (Ch 3, 4).** Do: Assignment 1 Q5, Q6, Q7. Sit the **Class Test** at the end of the week.

**Week 5: Partial derivatives, chain rule, gradient (Ch 5.1 to 5.3).** Do: Assignment 2 Q1, Q2.

**Week 6: Optimisation and Lagrange multipliers (Ch 5.4).** Do: Assignment 2 Q3. Checkpoint: classify five critical points with the second-derivative test.

**Week 7: Double integrals (Ch 6.1).** Do: Assignment 2 Q4. Practise sketching regions and switching the order of integration.

**Week 8: Triple integrals, cylindrical and spherical coordinates, change of variables (Ch 6.2, 6.3).** Do: Assignment 2 Q5, Q7. Mock midterm: first half of the Final Exam.

**Week 9: Vector fields, line integrals, Green's, Stokes', divergence theorems (Ch 7).** Do: Assignment 2 Q6.

**Week 10: First-order ODEs and modelling (Ch 8).** Do: Assignment 3 Q1, Q2.

**Week 11: Second-order linear ODEs and oscillations (Ch 9).** Do: Assignment 3 Q3, Q4.

**Week 12: Laplace transforms (Ch 10.1 to 10.3).** Do: Assignment 3 Q5(a) to (c). Memorise the table.

**Week 13: Laplace methods for IVPs, step and impulse inputs, transfer functions; systems of ODEs (Ch 10.4, 10.5, 11).** Do: Assignment 3 Q5(d), (e) and Q6. Simulate an RLC response in Python and compare with your solution.

**Week 14: Revision.** Redo missed Class Test and assignment questions, then sit the **Final Exam** under exam conditions (3 hours, formula sheet only).

## Milestone checklist
- [ ] I can pick and carry out the right integration technique and handle improper integrals.
- [ ] I can compute areas and lengths in parametric and polar forms.
- [ ] I can use dot and cross products to find angles, areas, lines and planes in 3D.
- [ ] I can compute partial derivatives, gradients and tangent planes, and optimise with and without constraints.
- [ ] I can set up and evaluate double and triple integrals in rectangular, polar, cylindrical and spherical coordinates.
- [ ] I can evaluate line and surface integrals and apply Green's, Stokes' and the divergence theorems.
- [ ] I can solve separable, linear and exact first-order ODEs and model with them.
- [ ] I can solve second-order linear ODEs (homogeneous, undetermined coefficients, variation of parameters).
- [ ] I can solve IVPs with the Laplace transform, including step and impulse inputs.
- [ ] I scored at least 70 percent on the Final Exam with a formula sheet only.

## Study habits that work
1. Draw the region or curve for every integral before writing limits.
2. After every ODE solution, substitute it back and test the initial conditions.
3. Keep a list of standard transforms and a "which method" flow-chart for each topic.
4. Verify with software after you have an answer, and investigate every disagreement.
5. Connect every method to engineering: Laplace transforms to circuits and control, vector calculus to electromagnetism, ODEs to vibrations and thermal systems.
