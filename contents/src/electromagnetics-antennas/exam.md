## Section B solutions

**B1.** Step 1: lambda = c/f = 2.998 × 10^8/(2.4 × 10^9) = **12.5 cm**.
Step 2: dielectric epsilon_r = 9: v = c/3 = **1.0 × 10^8 m/s**.
Step 3: lambda at 1 GHz = v/f = **10.0 cm**.
Step 4: eta = 376.7/3 = **125.6 Ω**.

**B2.** Step 1: copper at 1 GHz: delta = 1/sqrt(π f mu0 sigma) = 1/sqrt(π × 10^9 × 4π × 10^-7 × 5.8 × 10^7) = 1/sqrt(2.29 × 10^11) = **2.09 μm**.
Step 2: seawater at 1 MHz: delta = 1/sqrt(π × 10^6 × 4π × 10^-7 × 4) = **0.25 m**.
Step 3: at microwave frequencies the current flows only in a layer about 2 μm thick, so a plating of a few skin depths (5 to 10 μm) of a good conductor such as silver or gold on any base metal gives the same conductivity as a solid conductor.

**B3.** Step 1: Gamma = (100 - 50)/(100 + 50) = **0.333**.
Step 2: VSWR = (1 + 0.333)/(1 - 0.333) = **2.0**.
Step 3: return loss = 20 log10(3) = **9.54 dB**.

**B4.** Step 1: Z_in(lambda/8) = 50 (100 + j50)/(50 + j100) = 50 (100 + j50)(50 - j100)/12,500 = 50 (10,000 - j7500)/12,500 = **40 - j30 Ω**.

**B6.** R_r = **73.1 Ω**; D = **1.641 (2.15 dBi)**; HPBW = **78.1°**.

**B7.** Step 1: lambda = 3.0 cm; G = 0.6 (π × 1/0.02998)² = 0.6 × 10,981 = 6589 = **38.2 dBi**.

## Section C solutions

**C1.**
(a) eta2 = 188.4 Ω: Gamma = (188.4 - 376.7)/(188.4 + 376.7) = **-0.333**; tau = 1 + Gamma = **0.667**; power reflected = Gamma^2 = **11.1 percent**. Air to glass: Gamma = (1 - 1.5)/(1 + 1.5) = -0.2: **4 percent** reflected.
(b) Brewster angle = arctan(1.5) = **56.3°**; critical angle (glass to air) = arcsin(1/1.5) = **41.8°**.
(c) delta = 8.53 mm at 60 Hz, **66.1 μm** at 1 MHz, **2.09 μm** at 1 GHz: at power frequencies shielding by thin sheet is ineffective for magnetic fields (use thick, high-permeability metal), but at radio and microwave frequencies even thin foil (many skin depths) attenuates the field strongly by absorption and reflection.

**C2.**
(a) Z0 = sqrt(250 × 10^-9/100 × 10^-12) = **50 Ω**; v = 1/sqrt(L'C') = **2 × 10^8 m/s (0.667 c)**; coax Z0 = (60/1.5) ln(3.267) = **47.4 Ω**.
(b) 100 Ω: |Gamma| **0.333**, VSWR **2.0**, RL **9.54 dB**. 25 Ω: **0.333**, **2.0**, **9.54 dB**. 75 Ω: **0.200**, **1.5**, **13.98 dB**. 50 + j50 Ω: **0.447**, **2.62**, **6.99 dB**.
(c) Step 1: the load 100 + j50 Ω gives y_L = 1/(2 + j) = 0.4 - j0.2 (normalised).
Step 2: at **d = 0.1988 lambda** from the load the normalised admittance is 1 + j1.00: a shorted shunt stub of length **0.125 lambda** (susceptance -j1) matches.
Step 3: the second solution: **d = 0.375 lambda**, admittance 1 - j1.00 and a stub of **0.375 lambda**.

**C3.**
Step 1: WR-90: f_c(TE10) = **6.557 GHz**, TE20 = **13.114 GHz**, TE01 = **14.754 GHz** (single-mode range about 6.6 to 13.1 GHz).
Step 2: Table (lambda_g = lambda/sqrt(1 - (f_c/f)^2), v_p = c/sqrt(...), v_g = c sqrt(...), Z = eta0/sqrt(...)): **8 GHz**: 65.4 mm, 1.746, 0.573, 657.6 Ω.
Step 3: **10 GHz**: 39.7 mm, 1.324, 0.755, 499.0 Ω.
Step 4: **12 GHz**: 29.8 mm, 1.194, 0.838, 449.8 Ω.
Step 5: **v_p > c** because the phase velocity is the speed at which a phase front sweeps along the guide axis (the wave zigzags between the walls at an angle; the fronts intersect the axis faster than the wave travels); the energy and information travel at **v_g = c^2/v_p < c**.
Step 6: Waveguide versus coax: waveguide has low loss, high power capability and no radiation, but is bulky, expensive and has a cutoff (no DC, single-mode band); coax is flexible and broadband from DC but has higher loss and lower power limits.

**C4.**
(a) l = lambda/2: **1.50 m** at 100 MHz, **6.25 cm** at 2.4 GHz; R_r = **73.1 Ω**; D = **1.641 (2.15 dBi)**; HPBW **78.1°**; with a 5 Ω loss: eta = 73/78 = 0.936: G = 1.536 = **1.86 dBi**.
(b) R_r = 80 pi^2 (l/lambda)^2: **7.9 Ω** at lambda/10 and **0.32 Ω** at lambda/50: the loss resistance (several ohms in the conductor and matching circuit) becomes comparable to or larger than R_r, so efficiency is low; also a large capacitive reactance gives a high Q and narrow bandwidth.
(c) Step 1: D = N for lambda/2 spacing: **2, 4, 8, 16** (3, 6, 9, 12 dB).
Step 2: A phase gradient delta between elements steers the main beam to cos(theta_0) = -delta lambda/(2 pi d) electronically.
Step 3: **Grating lobes** are extra main beams that appear when d is above about lambda (or larger than lambda/(1 + |sin theta_max|) for scanned arrays): keep d <= lambda/2 for wide scan.

**C5.**
(a) FSPL = 20 log10(d_km) + 20 log10(f_MHz) + 32.44: **195.6 dB** (36,000 km at 4 GHz); **100.0 dB** (1 km at 2.4 GHz); **111.5 dB** (10 km at 900 MHz).
(b) P_r = 60 + 40 + 35 - 195.6 = **-60.6 dBm**.
(c) S = 1000/(4 pi 100^2) = **7.96 mW/m^2**; E_rms = sqrt(S eta0) = sqrt(7.96 × 10^-3 × 376.7) = **1.73 V/m**.
(d) Keep return paths short with a continuous ground plane; shield sensitive circuits and cables; filter (ferrites, LC) power and signal lines at connectors; use differential signalling and twisted pairs; limit edge rates and separate noisy digital from sensitive analog circuitry; decouple supplies near the pins; ESD protection and proper grounding.
