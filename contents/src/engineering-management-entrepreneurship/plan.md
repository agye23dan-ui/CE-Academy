# Engineering Management and Entrepreneurship: 14-Week Study Plan and Resources

Time budget: 8 to 11 hours per week (3 reading or lectures, 4 to 6 calculations and case work, 1 review with the app's quizzes). The course alternates between analytical tools (economics, scheduling, quality, decision analysis) and people/venture skills (leadership, customer discovery, financing, intellectual property). Apply every tool to a real or hypothetical product idea of your own; the final project is a business case and project plan for it.

## Textbooks
- Chan Park, *Contemporary Engineering Economics*; William Sullivan, Elin Wicks and Patrick Koelling, *Engineering Economy*; Leland Blank and Anthony Tarquin, *Basics of Engineering Economy*.
- Project Management Institute, *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK)*; Harold Kerzner, *Project Management: A Systems Approach*; Eliyahu Goldratt, *Critical Chain*.
- Douglas Montgomery, *Introduction to Statistical Quality Control*; Wayne Winston, *Operations Research*; Frederick Hillier and Gerald Lieberman, *Introduction to Operations Research*.
- Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup*; Steve Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*; Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur, *Business Model Generation*; Ash Maurya, *Running Lean*.
- Brad Feld and Jason Mendelson, *Venture Deals*; Aswath Damodaran's valuation notes (free online); Clayton Christensen, *The Innovator's Dilemma*.
- Roger Fisher and William Ury, *Getting to Yes*; Patrick Lencioni, *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team*; Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*.

## Free courses and resources
- MIT OpenCourseWare 15.390 New Enterprises and 15.401 Finance Theory; Stanford's "Startup Engineering" and Steve Blank's Udacity "How to Build a Startup".
- Y Combinator Startup School (free) and its library of fundraising and growth essays; Paul Graham's essays.
- WIPO and national patent office tutorials (Google Patents for prior-art search); Creative Commons and Open Source Initiative licence pages.
- Khan Academy finance and capital markets; Investopedia glossaries; Excel/Google Sheets templates for NPV, IRR and loan tables.
- ASQ (American Society for Quality) resources on control charts and Six Sigma; the Lean Enterprise Institute.

## Tools
A spreadsheet (NPV, IRR, PMT, XNPV functions), project-planning software (MS Project, ProjectLibre or Gantt templates), Python or R for control charts and simulation, Miro or Figma for canvases and prototypes, and a cap-table template.

## Week by week

**Week 1: Engineering management and leadership.** Management functions, organization structures, team development, motivation, communication and conflict. Case: diagnose the structure of a company you know.

**Week 2: Time value of money.** Compound interest, equivalence, annuities, loans, effective rates, inflation. Exercises: build an amortization table in a spreadsheet and verify the payment formula.

**Week 3: Evaluating projects.** NPV, IRR, payback, benefit-cost ratio, incremental analysis of mutually exclusive projects. Exercises: rank three alternatives and justify the choice with incremental IRR.

**Week 4: Depreciation, taxes, break-even and make-or-buy.** Straight line, DDB, SYD, MACRS, after-tax cash flows, cost-volume-profit analysis, sensitivity. Exercises: build an after-tax cash-flow model and a tornado chart.

**Week 5: Project planning.** Scope, WBS, estimating, critical path method, float, resource leveling. Exercises: draw a network and compute ES/EF/LS/LF for a 10-task project.

**Week 6: Schedule uncertainty and control.** PERT, crashing, critical chain, earned value management, forecasting. Exercises: compute CPI, SPI, EAC and TCPI and write a status report.

**Week 7: Midterm and risk.** Review. Risk registers, qualitative and quantitative risk analysis, agile versus waterfall, contracts and procurement.

**Week 8: Operations and inventory.** EOQ, safety stock, reorder points, queueing and staffing, lean and OEE. Exercises: size the service staff for a call centre with Erlang C.

**Week 9: Quality management.** Control charts (X-bar, R, p), process capability (C_p, C_pk), Six Sigma DMAIC, acceptance sampling, reliability and maintenance. Exercises: build control charts from data and decide whether the process is capable.

**Week 10: Decision analysis and entrepreneurship basics.** Decision trees, EMV, EVPI, multi-criteria decisions, opportunity recognition, customer discovery, the lean startup and the business model canvas. Exercises: interview five potential customers and record the findings.

**Week 11: Market, pricing and unit economics.** TAM/SAM/SOM, pricing and elasticity, LTV, CAC, churn, burn rate and runway, financial projections. Exercises: build a three-year model for your product.

**Week 12: Financing and valuation.** Funding sources, valuation methods, term sheets, cap tables and dilution, exits. Exercises: model seed and Series A rounds and compute founders' ownership and exit proceeds.

**Week 13: Intellectual property, law and ethics.** Patents, copyright, trademarks, trade secrets, licensing and open source, contracts, liability, professional ethics. Exercises: search prior art for your idea and write a one-page IP strategy.

**Week 14: Business case presentation and review.** Pitch your venture or business case (problem, solution, market, economics, plan, team, funding, risks) and revise using the feedback; past-paper revision.

## Assessment pattern used in this course
Three assignments (engineering economics; project management and quality; entrepreneurship, finance and intellectual property), a class test, and a three-hour final. Practise by rebuilding each worked example in a spreadsheet and then changing the assumptions.

## Study tips
- State every assumption (MARR, tax rate, timing of cash flows) and test how sensitive the decision is to it.
- Check cash-flow signs and timing first; most errors in economics come from putting flows in the wrong period.
- Use a risk register and a baseline schedule from day one, and update them weekly.
- Talk to customers before building: one conversation teaches more than a month of speculation.
- Keep a clean cap table and a record of who invented and owns what; fixing IP and equity problems later is expensive.
