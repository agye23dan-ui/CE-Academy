# Physics II (Electricity and Magnetism): 14-Week Study Plan and Resources

Time budget: 9 to 11 hours per week (3 reading, 5 to 6 problems, 1 review with the app's quizzes). This course is the physical foundation of circuits, electronics, signals and communication, so aim for fluency, not memorisation. Calculus II (vector calculus) is a co-requisite: study Chapters 5 to 7 of that course alongside Chapter 2 and Chapter 9 here.

## Textbooks
- Young and Freedman, *University Physics*, Volume 2 (Pearson). Clear, with many graded problems.
- Halliday, Resnick and Walker, *Fundamentals of Physics*, electromagnetism chapters.
- David Griffiths, *Introduction to Electrodynamics*. The best second-pass text, with Maxwell's equations in vector form.
- Purcell and Morin, *Electricity and Magnetism* (excellent physical insight).
- Agarwal and Lang, *Foundations of Analog and Digital Electronic Circuits*, and Nilsson and Riedel, *Electric Circuits*, for circuit methods.

## Free courses
- MIT OpenCourseWare 8.02 Physics II: Electricity and Magnetism (Walter Lewin's lectures and the TEAL course).
- MIT OpenCourseWare 6.002 Circuits and Electronics.
- PhET simulations: Charges and Fields, Circuit Construction Kit, Faraday's Law, Wave on a String, Radio Waves.
- Falstad's circuit simulator (free in the browser) and LTspice for circuit experiments.

## Week by week

**Week 1: Charge, Coulomb's law, electric fields (Ch 1, 2.1 to 2.3).** Do: Assignment 1 Q1 to Q3. Checkpoint: field of two point charges at any point.

**Week 2: Gauss's law and conductors (Ch 2.4).** Do: Assignment 1 Q4, Q5. Practise recognising the three symmetries.

**Week 3: Electric potential and energy (Ch 3).** Do: Assignment 1 Q6, Q7.

**Week 4: Capacitors and dielectrics (Ch 4).** Do: Assignment 1 Q8. Sit the **Class Test** at the end of the week.

**Week 5: Current, resistance, power, DC circuits (Ch 5.1, 5.2).** Do: Assignment 2 Q1, Q2, Q6, Q7. Build circuits in a simulator and compare with calculations.

**Week 6: Kirchhoff's laws, node and mesh methods, Thevenin (Ch 5.3).** Do: Assignment 2 Q3, Q5.

**Week 7: RC circuits and practical design (Ch 5.4).** Do: Assignment 2 Q4, Q8. Build an RC timer on a breadboard and measure τ with an oscilloscope.

**Week 8: Magnetic forces and sources of B (Ch 6).** Do: Assignment 3 Q1, Q2. Mock midterm: first half of the Final Exam.

**Week 9: Faraday's law, Lenz's law, generators, motors (Ch 7.1).** Do: Assignment 3 Q3.

**Week 10: Inductance, RL, LC circuits, transformers (Ch 7.2, 7.3).** Do: Assignment 3 Q4, Q8.

**Week 11: AC circuits, phasors, impedance (Ch 8.1, 8.2).** Do: Assignment 3 Q5 (a) to (c). Practise complex arithmetic by hand.

**Week 12: AC power, resonance, filters (Ch 8.3, 8.4).** Do: Assignment 3 Q5 (d). Plot |H(f)| of RC and RLC filters in Python.

**Week 13: Maxwell's equations and electromagnetic waves (Ch 9).** Do: Assignment 3 Q6, Q7. Write each equation in words and give two applications.

**Week 14: Revision.** Redo missed Class Test and assignment questions, then sit the **Final Exam** under exam conditions (3 hours, formula sheet only).

## Milestone checklist
- [ ] I can find E and V for point charges, rings, lines, sheets and spheres.
- [ ] I can use Gauss's law with spherical, cylindrical and planar symmetry, including conductors.
- [ ] I can analyse capacitor networks and energy, with and without dielectrics.
- [ ] I can solve resistor networks using series/parallel rules, KCL/KVL, node voltages and Thevenin.
- [ ] I can analyse RC and RL transients and use time constants.
- [ ] I can apply the Lorentz force, Biot-Savart and Ampère's laws.
- [ ] I can apply Faraday's and Lenz's laws to coils, generators and motional EMF.
- [ ] I can analyse series and parallel AC circuits with phasors, find power and power factor, and describe resonance.
- [ ] I can state Maxwell's four equations and describe electromagnetic waves quantitatively.
- [ ] I scored at least 70 percent on the Final Exam with a formula sheet only.

## Study habits that work
1. For every field problem, draw the geometry, choose the symmetry, and decide which law (Coulomb, Gauss, Biot-Savart, Ampère) is quickest before calculating.
2. Estimate magnitudes (is 10^9 N/C realistic?) and check units at each step.
3. Always verify circuit answers by checking power balance and Kirchhoff's laws.
4. Build and measure: use a multimeter and oscilloscope on real circuits, and compare with predictions.
5. Keep an equation sheet organised by topic, with a sketch of each configuration.
