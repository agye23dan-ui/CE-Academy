## Section B solutions

**B1.** At the midpoint r = 0.15 m from each. The fields point away from each positive charge, so they oppose: E = k(3.0 - 1.0) × 10^-9/(0.15)^2 = 8.99 × 10^9 · 2.0 × 10^-9/0.0225 = **799 N/C**, directed **toward the weaker (1.0 nC) charge**.

**B2.** Step 1: The series pair: (4 · 12)/(4 + 12) = 3.0 μF. In parallel with 2.0 μF: **C_eq = 5.0 μF**.
Step 2: At 10 V: the series branch carries Q = 3.0 μF · 10 V = **30 μC** on each of the 4 μF and 12 μF capacitors (voltages 7.5 V and 2.5 V); the 2.0 μF capacitor carries **20 μC**.
Step 3: Total 50 μC = 5.0 μF · 10 V ✓.

**B3.** Step 1: A = π(0.25 × 10^-3)^2 = 1.963 × 10^-7 m^2.
Step 2: R = ρ L/A = 1.1 × 10^-6 · 10/1.963 × 10^-7 = **56.0 Ω**.
Step 3: P = V^2/R = 14400/56.0 = **257 W**.

**B4.** Step 1: I = ε/(r + R) = 12/(1.0 + 3.0 + 8.0) = **1.0 A**.
Step 2: V_terminal = 12 - 1.0 · 1.0 = **11 V**.
Step 3: P_3Ω = I^2 R = **3.0 W**.

**B5.** Step 1: τ = R C = 20,000 · 100 × 10^-6 = 2.0 s. v(3.0 s) = 50 e^(-1.5) = **11.2 V**.
Step 2: The energy is proportional to v^2, which decays as e^(-2t/τ).
Step 3: U/U0 = 0.10: e^(-2t/τ) = 0.10, t = (τ/2) ln 10 = **2.30 s** (Initial energy (1/2)(100 × 10^-6)(2500) = 0.125 J.)

**B6.** F = I L B sin θ = 5.0 · 0.40 · 0.30 · sin 30° = **0.30 N**.

**B7.** ω = 2π · 50 = 314 rad/s. ε_max = N A B ω = 50 · 0.040 · 0.50 · 314 = **314 V**; ε_rms = 314/sqrt 2 = **222 V**.

**B8.** f_c = 1/(2π R C) = 1/(2π · 1000 · 159 × 10^-9) = **1001 Hz**. |H| = 1/sqrt(1 + (f/f_c)^2) = 1/sqrt(1 + 100) = **0.0995**, which is **-20.0 dB** (a decade above cut-off the first-order filter falls by 20 dB).

## Section C solutions

**C1.**
(a) Take a Gaussian cylinder of radius r (a < r < b) and length L: E (2π r L) = λ L/ε0, so **E = λ/(2π ε0 r)**, radially outward.
(b) V = ∫(a to b) E dr = (λ/(2π ε0)) ln(b/a). C per metre = λ/V = **2π ε0/ln(b/a) = 2π(8.854 × 10^-12)/ln 4 = 40.1 pF/m**.
(c) Step 1: λ = C' V = 40.1 × 10^-12 · 100 = **4.01 nC/m**.
Step 2: E is largest at the inner conductor (r = a): E_max = V/(a ln(b/a)) = 100/(1.0 × 10^-3 · 1.386) = **7.2 × 10^4 V/m**, far below air breakdown (3 × 10^6 V/m).
Step 3: A thicker centre conductor or a smaller ratio b/a would raise the capacitance; the design of coaxial cables sets the characteristic impedance by b/a.

**C2.**
(a) KCL at the top node with potential V (currents entering the node): from branch 1, (10 - V)/2.0; from branch 2 (the source's positive terminal is at the bottom, so the branch EMF is -4.0 V relative to the top): (-4.0 - V)/6.0; leaving through branch 3: V/4.0. (10 - V)/2 + (-4 - V)/6 = V/4. Multiply by 12: 6(10 - V) + 2(-4 - V) = 3V, so 60 - 6V - 8 - 2V = 3V, 52 = 11V and **V = 4.727 V**.
(b) I1 = (10 - 4.727)/2 = **2.636 A** into the node; I3 = 4.727/4 = **1.182 A** down through the 4 Ω; I2 = (-4 - 4.727)/6 = **-1.455 A**, i.e. **1.455 A flows out of the top node, down through branch 2**, entering the 4 V source at its negative terminal and leaving at the positive terminal (so this source delivers power). Check: 2.636 - 1.455 = 1.182 ✓.
(c) Step 1: Source 1 supplies 10 · 2.636 = **26.4 W**.
Step 2: Source 2 supplies 4 · 1.455 = **5.82 W**.
Step 3: Dissipated: R1: 2.636^2 · 2 = 13.9 W; R2: 1.455^2 · 6 = 12.7 W; R3: 1.182^2 · 4 = 5.59 W; total **32.2 W**, which equals 26.4 + 5.82 = 32.2 W ✓.

**C3.**
(a) (1/2) m v^2 = e V: m = 40 · 1.6605 × 10^-27 = 6.642 × 10^-26 kg. v = sqrt(2 e V/m) = sqrt(2 · 1.602 × 10^-19 · 2000/6.642 × 10^-26) = sqrt(9.648 × 10^9) = **9.82 × 10^4 m/s**.
(b) r = m v/(e B) = 6.642 × 10^-26 · 9.82 × 10^4/(1.602 × 10^-19 · 0.50) = **0.0814 m (8.14 cm)**.
(c) Step 1: For 38 u: m = 6.310 × 10^-26 kg.
Step 2: Since r = (1/B) sqrt(2 m V/e) ∝ sqrt(m): r = 0.0814 · sqrt(38/40) = **0.0794 m**.
Step 3: The ions land at 2r from the entry slit: the separation of the landing points is 2(0.0814 - 0.0794) = **4.1 mm**, easily resolved by a detector.

**C4.**
(a) ω = 2π · 100 = 628.3 rad/s. X_L = ω L = **62.8 Ω**; X_C = 1/(ω C) = 1/(628.3 · 10^-5) = **159.2 Ω**. |Z| = sqrt(20^2 + (62.8 - 159.2)^2) = sqrt(400 + 9277) = **98.4 Ω**. φ = arctan((62.8 - 159.2)/20) = **-78.3°**: net capacitive, so the **current leads the voltage**.
(b) Step 1: I = 50/98.4 = **0.508 A**.
Step 2: P = I^2 R = 0.258 · 20 = **5.17 W**.
Step 3: Power factor = R/|Z| = **0.203**.
(c) Step 1: f0 = 1/(2π sqrt(LC)) = 1/(2π · 10^-3) = **159 Hz** (ω0 = 1000 rad/s).
Step 2: Q = ω0 L/R = 1000 · 0.10/20 = **5.0**.
Step 3: At resonance I = V/R = **2.5 A**, V_L = I ω0 L = 2.5 · 100 = **250 V** (five times the source voltage, by the quality factor; the capacitor voltage is also 250 V).

**C5.**
(a) Step 1: I = P/(4π r^2) = 1000/(4π · 10^8) = **7.96 × 10^-7 W/m^2**.
Step 2: I = E_max^2/(2 μ0 c) = (1/2) ε0 c E_max^2, so E_max = sqrt(2I/(ε0 c)) = sqrt(2 · 7.96 × 10^-7/(8.854 × 10^-12 · 3.00 × 10^8)) = **0.0245 V/m**.
Step 3: B_max = E_max/c = **8.16 × 10^-11 T**.
(b) λ = c/f = **3.0 m**; half-wave dipole length = λ/2 = **1.5 m**; delay t = 10^4/3.00 × 10^8 = **33.3 μs**.
(c) Step 1: Ampère-Maxwell law: ∮ B · dl = μ0 I_enc + μ0 ε0 dΦ_E/dt.
Step 2: In a wave in vacuum there is no conduction current, so the changing electric flux alone produces a circulating magnetic field; by Faraday's law the changing magnetic flux in turn produces a circulating electric field.
Step 3: Each field regenerates the other, and the resulting self-sustaining disturbance propagates at c = 1/sqrt(μ0 ε0), with E/B = c and E, B, and the direction of propagation mutually perpendicular.
