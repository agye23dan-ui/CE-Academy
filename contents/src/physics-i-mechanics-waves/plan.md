# Physics I (Mechanics and Waves): 14-Week Study Plan and Resources

Time budget: 9 to 11 hours per week (3 reading, 5 to 6 problems, 1 review with the app's quizzes). Physics rewards problem-solving practice far more than re-reading: aim for at least 8 full problems per week.

## Textbooks
- Young and Freedman, *University Physics with Modern Physics* (Pearson). The standard engineering text with graded problems.
- Halliday, Resnick and Walker, *Fundamentals of Physics*. Clear explanations and many conceptual questions.
- Kleppner and Kolenkow, *An Introduction to Mechanics*. Harder and deeper; use for the second pass.
- Feynman, *Lectures on Physics* Volume I (free online). For insight, not drill.

## Free courses
- MIT OpenCourseWare 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (lectures, problem sets, exams with solutions).
- MIT OpenCourseWare 8.03SC Physics III: Vibrations and Waves (for the wave chapters).
- Walter Lewin's 8.01 lectures (demonstrations that build intuition).
- PhET interactive simulations (University of Colorado) for projectiles, springs, waves and collisions.

## Week by week

**Week 1: Units, vectors, 1D kinematics (Ch 1, 2).** Objectives: use dimensional analysis; solve constant-acceleration problems. Do: Assignment 1 Q1, Q2. Checkpoint: derive the four kinematic equations from a = constant.

**Week 2: 2D kinematics, projectiles, relative motion, circular motion (Ch 3).** Do: Assignment 1 Q3, Q4, Q7.

**Week 3: Newton's laws and friction (Ch 4.1 to 4.3).** Do: Assignment 1 Q5, Q6, Q8. Habit: a free-body diagram for every problem.

**Week 4: Dynamics review, drag and terminal velocity (Ch 4).** Sit the **Class Test** (60 minutes). Fix every missed question before moving on.

**Week 5: Work, energy and power (Ch 5).** Do: Assignment 2 Q1 to Q3. Checkpoint: solve a loop-the-loop problem unaided.

**Week 6: Momentum, impulse and collisions (Ch 6).** Do: Assignment 2 Q4 to Q6.

**Week 7: Rotation: kinematics, torque, moment of inertia (Ch 7.1 to 7.3).** Do: Assignment 2 Q7. Checkpoint: derive I for a rod and a disc by integration.

**Week 8: Angular momentum, equilibrium, gravitation (Ch 7.4, 7.5, 8).** Do: Assignment 2 Q8 to Q10. Mock midterm: first half of the Final Exam.

**Week 9: Simple harmonic motion (Ch 9.1).** Do: Assignment 3 Q1, Q2. Use a stopwatch to measure your own pendulum and compare with 2π sqrt(L/g).

**Week 10: Damped and driven oscillations, resonance (Ch 9.2, 9.3).** Do: Assignment 3 Q3, Q4. Plot A(ω) for three values of the damping.

**Week 11: Travelling waves and the wave equation (Ch 10.1).** Do: Assignment 3 Q5.

**Week 12: Superposition, interference, standing waves (Ch 10.2, 10.3).** Do: Assignment 3 Q6, Q9.

**Week 13: Sound, intensity, Doppler effect (Ch 10.4).** Do: Assignment 3 Q7, Q8.

**Week 14: Revision.** Redo missed Class Test and assignment questions, then sit the **Final Exam** under exam conditions (3 hours, formula sheet only).

## Milestone checklist
- [ ] I can solve constant-acceleration and projectile problems with correct sign conventions.
- [ ] I can draw a correct free-body diagram and solve connected-body problems with friction.
- [ ] I can choose between force, energy and momentum methods for a problem.
- [ ] I can analyse 1D and 2D collisions.
- [ ] I can use torque, moment of inertia and angular momentum conservation.
- [ ] I can compute orbital speeds, periods and energies.
- [ ] I can write and fit an SHM solution, and explain damping and resonance.
- [ ] I can analyse standing waves on strings and in pipes, and apply the Doppler effect.
- [ ] I scored at least 70 percent on the Final Exam with a formula sheet only.

## Study habits that work
1. Solve symbolically first, then plug in numbers. Check units and limiting cases (what if m → 0, or θ → 0?).
2. Always ask whether the result is plausible (a car's stopping distance of 3 km is a red flag).
3. Redo each example problem a week later with the solution covered.
4. Verify a few problems numerically in Python (scipy.integrate.solve_ivp) to see the physics evolve.
5. Keep a one-page formula sheet that you write yourself.
