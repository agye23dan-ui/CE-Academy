# Physics I (Mechanics and Waves): Final Examination

Duration: 3 hours. Calculator and a one-page formula sheet allowed. Use g = 9.8 m/s^2, v_sound = 343 m/s, G M_E = 3.986 × 10^14 m^3/s^2, R_E = 6.371 × 10^6 m. Total: 100 marks.
- Section A: 20 multiple-choice questions, 1 mark each (20 marks). Answer all.

## Section A: Multiple Choice (20 marks)

A) 1 m/s  B) 2 m/s  C) 4 m/s  D) 8 m/s

**10.** The moment of inertia of a uniform rod (mass M, length L) about one end is

**11.** For a solid sphere rolling without slipping, the fraction of the kinetic energy that is rotational is

**12.** The ratio of escape speed to circular orbital speed at the same radius is

**16.** A driven lightly damped oscillator has maximum amplitude when the drive frequency is

## Section C: Long Questions (answer any four, 10 marks each)

## Section B solutions

**B1.** Step 1: t = sqrt(2h/g) = sqrt(2 × 1.2/9.8) = sqrt(0.245) = **0.495 s**.
Step 2: x = v t = 3.0 × 0.495 = **1.48 m**.
Step 3: v_y = g t = 4.85 m/s, so impact speed = sqrt(3.0² + 4.85²) = **5.70 m/s** (check: sqrt(9 + 2 × 9.8 × 1.2) = 5.70 ✓).

**B2.** Equilibrium along the slope: k x = m g sin 30°, so x = (2.0 × 9.8 × 0.5)/100 = 9.8/100 = **0.098 m (9.8 cm)**.

**B3.** Step 1: a = μ g = 7.84 m/s² (μ = 0.80).
Step 2: d = v²/(2a) = 400/15.68 = **25.5 m**.
Step 3: W_friction = -(1/2) m v² = -(1/2)(1000)(400) = **-2.0 × 10^5 J**.

**B4.** Step 1: momentum: 60(3.0) = 120 v_boat, so the boat recoils at **1.5 m/s** (in the opposite direction).
Step 2: kinetic energy: (1/2)(60)(9) + (1/2)(120)(2.25) = 270 + 135 = **405 J** (supplied by the person's muscles).

**B5.** Step 1: g' = G M'/R'² = g (1/10)/(1/4) = 0.4 g = **3.92 m/s²**.
Step 2: weight = 70 × 3.92 = **274 N** (versus 686 N on Earth).

**B6.** Step 1: the tension is central, so angular momentum is conserved: m v r = m v' r', so v' = v (r/r') = 2.0 × 2 = **4.0 m/s**.
Step 2: K_i = (1/2)(0.2)(4) = 0.40 J, K_f = (1/2)(0.2)(16) = 1.60 J.
Step 3: **ΔK = +1.20 J**, which equals the work done by the person pulling the string.

**B7.** Step 1: ω = 20 rad/s, A = 0.050 m. m = k/ω² = 80/400 = **0.20 kg**.
Step 2: v_max = A ω = 0.050 × 20 = **1.0 m/s**.
Step 3: E = (1/2) k A² = (1/2)(80)(0.0025) = **0.10 J** (check: (1/2)(0.2)(1) = 0.10 ✓).

**B8.** Because x and t have the same sign, the wave travels in the **-x direction**. k = 10 rad/m so **λ = 2π/10 = 0.628 m**. ω = 200 rad/s so **f = 31.8 Hz**. **v = ω/k = 20 m/s** (check: f λ = 31.8 · 0.628 = 20 ✓).

## Section C solutions

**C1.** m1 = 5.0 kg on the incline, m2 = 4.0 kg hanging.
(a) The hanging weight is 39.2 N. The down-slope component on m1 is 5(9.8)(0.5) = 24.5 N, and the maximum friction is 0.10 · 5 · 9.8 cos 30° = 4.24 N. Since 39.2 > 24.5 + 4.24, the hanging mass descends and the block **moves up the slope**; kinetic friction acts down the slope. (1 mark each for the free-body diagrams and the decision.)
(b) Net driving force = 39.2 - 24.5 - 4.24 = 10.46 N. a = 10.46/(5 + 4) = **1.16 m/s^2**.
(c) For m2: m2 g - T = m2 a, so T = 4(9.8 - 1.162) = **34.6 N**. Check on m1: T - 24.5 - 4.24 = 5.81 = 5 a ✓.
(d) v = sqrt(2 a d) = sqrt(2 · 1.162 · 2.0) = **2.16 m/s**.
(e) Step 1: Work by gravity on m2: 4(9.8)(2) = 78.4 J.
Step 2: Gravity on m1: -5(9.8)(1.0) = -49 J (it rises 2 sin 30° = 1 m).
Step 3: Friction: -4.24 · 2 = -8.49 J.
Step 4: Net = 20.9 J = (1/2)(9)(2.156)^2 = 20.9 J ✓.

**C2.**
(a) U = (1/2) k x^2 = (1/2)(800)(0.0225) = **9.0 J**.
(b) Friction loss = μ m g d = 0.40 · 0.5 · 9.8 · 1.0 = 1.96 J. K = 9.0 - 1.96 = 7.04 J, so v = sqrt(2 · 7.04/0.5) = **5.31 m/s**.
(c) h = K/(m g) = 7.04/(0.5 · 9.8) = **1.44 m**.
(d) Step 1: A loop of radius R is completed if the kinetic energy at the bottom is at least (5/2) m g R = 2.5(0.5)(9.8)(0.5) = 6.125 J.
Step 2: Since 7.04 J > 6.125 J, **yes, it completes the loop** (with 0.9 J to spare).
Step 3: At the top, v^2 = v_bottom^2 - 4 g R = 28.16 - 19.6 = 8.56 m^2/s^2 ≥ g R = 4.9 ✓.

**C3.** M = 2.0 kg, L = 1.0 m.
(a) I = M L^2/3 = **0.667 kg m^2**.
(b) Torque at release = M g (L/2) = 9.8 N m. α = τ/I = 9.8/0.667 = **14.7 rad/s^2** (= 3g/(2L)).
(c) Acceleration of the centre of mass at release = α L/2 = 7.35 m/s^2 downward (no centripetal term as ω = 0). Then M g - N = M a_cm, so N = 2(9.8 - 7.35) = **4.9 N upward** (a quarter of the weight).
(d) Energy: M g L/2 = (1/2) I ω^2, so ω = sqrt(3g/L) = sqrt(29.4) = **5.42 rad/s**. Tip speed = ω L = **5.42 m/s**.
(e) A ball dropped through the same 1.0 m would reach sqrt(2 g L) = 4.43 m/s, slower than the tip (5.42 m/s). A rigid rod must share a single angular acceleration, so the centre of mass accelerates at only 0.75 g (7.35 m/s^2) while the tip, twice as far from the pivot, accelerates at 1.5 g (14.7 m/s^2), faster than free fall.

**C4.**
(a) Step 1: T = 2π sqrt(r³/(G M)), so r³ = G M T²/(4π²) = 3.986 × 10^14 × (86,164)²/39.478 = 7.50 × 10^22 m³.
Step 2: **r = 4.216 × 10^7 m**.
Step 3: altitude = r - R_E = **3.58 × 10^7 m (35,800 km)**.
(b) v = 2π r/T = sqrt(G M/r) = **3.07 km/s**.
(c) ΔE = m [G M/R_E - G M/(2r)] = 1000[6.256 × 10^7 - 4.73 × 10^6] = 1000 · 5.78 × 10^7 = **5.78 × 10^10 J**.
(d) The orbit's plane must contain Earth's centre. Only an equatorial orbit can keep a satellite fixed over one point on the ground (any inclination would make it trace a figure-eight).

**C5.**
(a) Step 1: v = sqrt(T/μ) = sqrt(80/0.0020) = sqrt(40,000) = **200 m/s**.
Step 2: f1 = v/(2L) = 200/(2 × 0.65) = 200/1.3 = **153.8 Hz**.
Step 3: f3 = 3 f1 = **461.5 Hz**.
(b) I = P/(4π r^2) = 0.50/(4π · 100) = **3.98 × 10^-4 W/m^2**. β = 10 log10(3.98 × 10^-4/10^-12) = 10(8.60) = **86.0 dB**.
(c) Observer approaching a stationary source: f' = f (v + v_o)/v = 153.8 · 353/343 = **158.3 Hz**.
(d) Closed pipe fundamental f = v/(4L), so L = 343/(4 · 153.8) = **0.557 m**.
